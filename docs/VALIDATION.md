# 15-minute user test kit

Judges ask "has anyone used it?" One real quote beats any feature. This kit gets you one in a week.

## Who to ask (pick 2 or 3)

- **A resident who reads one of the bulletin languages** (Urdu, Tamil, Cantonese, Tagalog, Russian...):
  a friend, a neighbour, a classmate. Best single test.
- **A Community Police Liaison Committee (CPLC) member** or anyone who runs a tenant association or
  community WhatsApp group: they are the ones who would forward it.
- **A settlement or community agency worker** (neighbourhood office, library branch, community health
  centre): they know what newcomers can and can't read.
- **A community translator**: ask them to review one translation on the Translations page.

Use the public link (`scripts/demo_tunnel.sh --background --hours 4`, or the permanent site in
`README.md`). Don't show staff pages unless you're testing the officer flow.

## Before you start (say this)

> "This is a student prototype built on Toronto Police open data. It isn't from the police. I'm testing
> the tool, not you. There are no wrong answers. Can I write down what you say, without your name?"

## Five tasks (watch, don't help)

1. "Find your neighbourhood and tell me what happened there last month." (Home → Find your neighbourhood)
2. "Open it in the language you'd read at home. Is anything wrong or strange?" (note exact words)
3. "Would you send this to someone? Show me how." (WhatsApp / QR)
4. "Tell the officers what they should focus on next month." (feedback form)
5. "Here's a sentence from a newsletter. Is it true?" (Check my numbers, example text)

Time each task. Note where they hesitate or ask you a question.

## Five questions

1. What did you understand this month's bulletin to say? (checks plain language)
2. What would you do with it? Who would you send it to?
3. Was anything confusing, missing or wrong, especially in your language?
4. Did anything make your neighbourhood look bad or unfair?
5. On a scale of 1 to 5, how likely are you to read it next month? Why?

## What to write down

- Their role (not their name), the language they used, time per task, and **one sentence in their own words**.
- Every translation problem, word for word (fix it on the Translations page and mark it reviewed).
- Ask: "Can I quote that sentence, without your name, in a presentation?" Only use quotes they agree to.

## Turning it into a slide

> "Tested with 3 people in 2 languages. 'I would send this to my building's WhatsApp group; the Urdu
> reads naturally.' (resident, Thorncliffe Park). Two translation fixes made from their feedback."

(Example wording only: use what people actually said.) Add results to `TEST_REPORT.md` under a
"User testing" heading, and the quote to `PITCH.md`.
