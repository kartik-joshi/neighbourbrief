Privacy and how AI is used

NeighbourBrief is an independent prototype built on open data. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Toronto Police Service.

What AI does, and what it never does

  • Numbers come from code, not AI. Counts are calculated directly from Toronto Police Service open data. The AI writer only phrases sentences around them.
  • Every number is checked in English and in every language before publishing; a translation is also read back in English to make sure "down" didn't become "up". If anything doesn't match, publishing is blocked.
  • A person is responsible. The officer reviews and edits every word; teams can require a second person to sign off.
  • Translations are labelled as AI-assisted, with a note that they may contain mistakes, until a human translator reviews them. The checks cover every number and whether each line keeps its meaning and direction; they can't promise the wording is perfect.
  • The AI never predicts crime, profiles people or groups, ranks neighbourhoods, or decides anything about anyone.
  • By default the writer is a model running on the officer's own computer (nothing is sent anywhere). Translation can use Microsoft Azure Translator; it receives only the bulletin text, which contains no personal information about residents.

Privacy

  • No personal information about residents is collected, stored or published, and no incident locations are shown: counts only, by neighbourhood.
  • No accounts, no cookies, no tracking. The tool runs on one computer and answers only that computer.
  • The only names in a bulletin are Neighbourhood Community Officers as Toronto Police publishes them in "Find My NCO". Names typed for a bulletin's history (author, reviewer) stay on that computer and are never printed.
  • "Use my location" is worked out inside your browser; your position is never sent or stored.
  • When online, pages load fonts from Google Fonts and map pages load map images from OpenStreetMap; both see your internet address, as with any website. In offline mode nothing leaves the computer.

The data

Toronto Police Service and City of Toronto open data, under the Open Government Licence – Ontario and Open Government Licence – Toronto. A report to police is not proof that a crime happened or that anyone was charged. See How we count and Data health.
