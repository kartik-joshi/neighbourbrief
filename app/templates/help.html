Help

Your first bulletin in five steps

  1. 1. Pick the neighbourhood. Type its name on Make a bulletin, or use "Use my location". The division, your officers, the data date and the suggested languages fill in.
  2. 2. Add your words. This month's priority and what you did, in a sentence or two each. If you made a bulletin last month, say what happened since ("you said, we did"). Optional: a prevention tip, the next meeting, who prepared it.
  3. 3. Draft and review. NeighbourBrief counts the reports from open data and drafts a one-page bulletin. Every number is underlined and checked; change any wording you like. If a number turns red, it doesn't match the data.
  4. 4. Translate. Pick the languages (suggested from the 2021 Census). Each translation is checked for the same numbers, the right alphabet, and the same meaning when read back in English.
  5. 5. Publish and share. Print one page per language, save PDFs, copy for WhatsApp or a newsletter. Large print is one click away.

Common questions

Publishing is blocked. What do I do?

The Publish page lists what failed. A number that doesn't match: fix the wording on Review. A translation problem: use "Translate again" on that language, or edit it by hand. Safety wording (for example a statement about a group of people): change it on Review. If you've checked it yourself, you can confirm a manual review and publish.

Why is a percentage missing?

Percent change is shown only when both numbers are 10 or more; small numbers jump around and a big percentage would mislead. Shootings and homicides are always counts only.

The numbers look lower than I expected.

Counts are reports to police by the date they were reported, for the last complete month. Very recent reports can still be added; the Review page warns when the data may not cover the whole month. TPS also reloads its data every day, and NeighbourBrief never uses data caught in the middle of a reload.

Can a community translator check a translation?

Yes: Translations to review shows each line side by side with the English. Once they confirm, the bulletin says it was reviewed by a human translator.

Does my team need a second person to approve?

It's optional. Anyone other than the author can sign off on the Publish page; if the text changes afterwards, the sign-off needs to be redone. Your administrator can make it required (REQUIRE_REVIEW=true) and rename the roles (for example Officer and Sergeant).

There's no internet at the meeting venue.

Before you go, open Status and save your neighbourhood for offline use. With offline mode on, drafting, checking and printing all work on the laptop alone.

Someone asks where a number comes from.

Each bulletin has "The numbers behind this bulletin" (a spreadsheet), and each neighbourhood profile links every count to the same query on the Toronto Police Service open data portal.

Can I just ask a question?

Yes: Ask about a neighbourhood answers in any language from the checked data only, with the sources linked. If an answer contains a number that isn't in the sources, it isn't shown. It never ranks neighbourhoods.

Can I check someone else's newsletter or post?

Check my numbers checks every number in any text against the open data for a neighbourhood and month.
